Yes — **there is the outline of a moat here, but the repository does not have a defensible moat yet**.

The important distinction is between **a differentiated architecture** and **a durable competitive advantage**.

### My assessment

| Moat component                    | Current position       | Why                                                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Novel problem framing             | **Strong**             | Treating verified human attention as an infrastructure primitive is differentiated |
| Protocol / standards moat         | **Potentially strong** | VAP could become a portable verification standard                                  |
| Technical architecture            | **Moderate**           | Evidence → claim → confidence → verification → proof is coherent, but reproducible |
| Proprietary data                  | **Weak currently**     | No demonstrated proprietary attention dataset yet                                  |
| ML moat                           | **Weak currently**     | Models are planned, but models themselves are not yet defensible                   |
| Network effects                   | **Not yet**            | Requires publishers, verifiers and consumers actually using the protocol           |
| Switching costs                   | **Not yet**            | There is no installed ecosystem to make switching costly                           |
| Cryptographic/provenance moat     | **Moderate potential** | Useful for independent verification, but cryptography alone isn't a moat           |
| Distribution                      | **Weak currently**     | SDKs and developer portal are still planned                                        |
| Regulatory/industry certification | **Potentially strong** | Could become significant if VAE becomes an accepted verification standard          |
| Overall moat today                | **Early / potential**  | Interesting architecture, but not yet difficult to replicate                       |

The repository itself is unusually explicit about wanting to be infrastructure rather than another attention analytics product. Its core primitive is **Evidence**, with claims, confidence, verification and signed Proofs of Attention derived from it. 

That is the part I find strategically interesting.

## The biggest problem: the idea is no longer uncontested

I searched the current landscape specifically around verified attention / Proof of Attention.

There are already projects pursuing substantially overlapping territory:

* **ATTEX**, spun out of Tobii, is building a human-attention database based on real eye-tracking and describes itself as a ground-truth dataset for online attention. It reports 20M+ eye-tracked impressions annually. ([attexdata.ai][1])
* The advertising industry itself is moving toward standardized attention measurement. IAB Tech Lab says the IAB/MRC Attention Measurement Guidelines were released in November 2025 and describes OM SDK as infrastructure for standardized, auditable measurement signals. ([IAB Tech Lab][2])
* **Amplified's AttentionAI** claims more than a decade of research and 50 billion real-human attention data points for predictive attention modeling. ([amplified.co][3])
* There are also several crypto/Web3 projects explicitly using the term **Proof of Attention**, including systems based on interaction evidence and on-chain rewards. ([GitHub][4])
* A newer project, **Ponta**, explicitly positions itself around verifying real attention rather than simply measuring impressions. ([ponta.co][5])

So **"we verify human attention" by itself is not the moat**.

# Where I think the real moat could be

The strongest idea in this repository isn't actually the ML.

It is this:

> **Create an interoperable evidence/proof layer that allows one party to establish an attention claim and another party to independently verify it.**

That is materially different from:

> "We have an attention score."

Your architecture explicitly separates:

**Observation → Evidence → Claim → Confidence → Verification → Proof of Attention → Applications**. 

That creates the possibility of a **trust/protocol moat**.

Think about the difference:

### Existing attention companies

```text
User
  ↓
Measurement provider
  ↓
Attention score
  ↓
Advertiser
```

The measurement provider owns the methodology and usually the data.

### VAE's proposed model

```text
                    ┌──────────────┐
Publisher ─────────►│              │
                    │    Evidence  │
Device/SDK ────────►│      ↓       │
                    │    Claim     │
Other signals ─────►│      ↓       │
                    │ Verification │
                    │      ↓       │
                    │    Proof     │
                    └──────┬───────┘
                           ↓
              ┌────────────┼────────────┐
              ↓            ↓            ↓
           Advertiser   Research     Education
```

If that becomes a genuinely **vendor-neutral verification standard**, the competitive dynamics change.

That is much closer to a real moat.

# The second moat: becoming the neutral verifier

This is potentially more important than the ML.

Suppose an advertiser says:

> "I want 10 million verified attention events."

A publisher says:

> "We generated 10 million."

Neither should have to trust the other.

Instead:

```text
Publisher
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
VAE verifier
    ↓
Signed PoA
    ↓
Advertiser
```

And critically, the advertiser can independently verify:

```text
verify(proof) → TRUE
```

Your repository already describes exactly this type of consumer-side verification. 

If enough organizations adopt that model, **the verifier itself becomes infrastructure**.

That is a much stronger position than being another analytics vendor.

# But there is one major weakness

Your biggest moat claim currently depends on something the repository does **not yet have**:

### **A credible way of proving attention rather than merely proving interaction.**

This distinction is enormous.

You can observe:

```text
mouse moved
scroll occurred
tab focused
article visible
video playing
keyboard activity
```

But none necessarily means:

> **the human was actually paying attention.**

That is the fundamental technical problem.

And competitors are already attacking it from different directions. ATTEX, for example, uses real eye-tracking data as ground truth, while the broader advertising ecosystem is developing attention measurement standards. ([attexdata.ai][1])

Therefore your moat cannot simply be:

> "We collect more browser signals."

That is relatively easy to copy.

It needs to be:

> **"We have demonstrated, independently validated models that infer attention from privacy-preserving evidence with known error bounds, and those proofs can be independently verified."**

That's considerably harder.

# What would turn this into a real moat?

I'd prioritize these five things:

### 1. Build the proprietary dataset

Not generic interaction logs.

Build:

**attention evidence → independently established ground truth → model outcome**

For example:

```text
10,000,000 sessions
       ↓
multi-signal evidence
       ↓
ground-truth subset
       ↓
attention labels
       ↓
calibrated model
       ↓
verification accuracy
```

This dataset becomes extremely difficult for a competitor to reproduce.

---

### 2. Establish measurable verification guarantees

Don't just output:

```json
{
  "attention": 0.87
}
```

Instead:

```json
{
  "claim": "human_attended_content",
  "confidence": 0.87,
  "evidence_class": "...",
  "policy": "...",
  "model_version": "...",
  "provenance": "...",
  "proof": "..."
}
```

And publish performance characteristics.

For example:

* false-positive rate
* false-negative rate
* calibration error
* adversarial robustness
* replay resistance
* bot resistance
* cross-device robustness
* cross-domain generalization

**A measurable verification standard is much harder to displace than a proprietary score.**

---

### 3. Make the protocol genuinely open

This is counterintuitive, but potentially important.

Don't make:

> "VAE is the only system that can verify attention."

Make:

> **"VAP defines how attention evidence and proofs are represented, and anyone can implement a verifier."**

Then VAE can become the **reference implementation and trusted ecosystem provider**.

Your repository already has the beginnings of this approach through the normative VAP specification and conformance testing. 

If universities, advertisers, AI companies, publishers and regulators start requiring **VAP-compatible proofs**, you've created a standards moat.

---

### 4. Build the network

The real flywheel would be:

```text
                MORE PUBLISHERS
                      ↓
                MORE EVIDENCE
                      ↓
                BETTER MODELS
                      ↓
               BETTER VERIFICATION
                      ↓
                 MORE TRUST
                      ↓
       ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
       ↓                             ↓
MORE CONSUMERS                 MORE VERIFIERS
       ↓                             ↓
       └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                      ↓
                MORE PUBLISHERS
```

That's the moat.

Not the TypeScript.

Not the API.

Not the cryptography.

Not even necessarily the ML.

**The ecosystem around the protocol is the moat.**

---

# One thing I'd change in the repository immediately

The README currently makes a very large claim:

> **"Establishing Verified Attention as a new layer of Internet infrastructure."** 

That's an ambitious destination, but the repository is currently still in active development, with several major components explicitly marked planned, including SDKs, ML serving, developer portal and marketplace. 

I would therefore distinguish very explicitly between:

**Current capability**

vs.

**Protocol specification**

vs.

**Target infrastructure**

vs.

**Future moat.**

Otherwise sophisticated investors/engineers may see the infrastructure language as premature.

---

## Bottom line

**Does the repo have a moat today?**

**No—not yet.**

**Does it contain the architecture for a potentially substantial moat?**

**Yes.**

And I think the most defensible version is **not "AI that measures attention."**

It is:

> **An interoperable evidence and verification protocol for proving human digital engagement, backed by proprietary ground-truth data, calibrated verification models, cryptographic provenance, and an ecosystem of independent publishers, verifiers and consumers.**

That combination could become difficult to replicate.

The most important strategic question for the project is therefore not *"Can we build VAE?"* — the repository suggests you can. It is:

**"What will VAE possess in 3 years that a well-funded competitor cannot simply reproduce by hiring 20 engineers?"**

The answer needs to be **data + validation + protocol adoption + network effects + trust**, rather than code alone.
